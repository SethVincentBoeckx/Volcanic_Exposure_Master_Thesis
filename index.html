Volcano population over time
GHSL (JRC) Wang et al. 2022 Point buffer
Population within circular radial buffers (10, 30, 100 km) centred on the volcano (coordinates from the Smithsonian GVP database). Historical data 1975–2030 from the GHSL (Global Human Settlement Layer, JRC / EU Commission). Future projections 2035–2100 from Wang et al. (2022) under the SSP2 scenario. The standardised view normalises all series to 1975 = 100.
Country population over time
GHSL (JRC) Wang et al. 2022 Point buffer Country-level aggregation
Total national population and cumulative buffer population (10, 30, 100 km) for all volcanoes within a country. Uses the same GHSL and Wang et al. time series as the volcano chart, aggregated at country level. Buffer populations are point-based circular radii. Standardised view uses 1975 = 100.
PEI & Weighted population
GHSL (JRC) Point Buffer PEI — Brown et al. 2015
The Population Exposure Index (PEI) is a categorical score (1–7) based on the Weighted population within 10, 30 and 100 km radius of the summit, using thresholds from Brown et al. (2015).
Convex hull vs. point buffer ratio
GHSL (JRC) Convex hull
Grosse et al. (2013) outer boundary polygons of monogenetic volcanoes + self drawn outer boundaries for polygenetic volcanoes taking the convex hull of all vents. Buffers of 10 / 30 / 100 km are applied around this polygon rather than the GVP point. The ratio (polygon buffer ÷ point buffer) shows how much the hull-based approach differs from a simple radius. Ratio > 1 means the polygon extends further than the volcano point.
2020 population — 10 km hull buffer
GHSL 2020 Convex hull + point-buffer 10 km ring
GHSL 2020 population within the 10 km buffer around the convex hull polygon including all vents, compared to the GVP point-based buffer. The buffered area includes both the interior of the volcanic outer polygon and a 10 km ring around its perimeter. All volcanoes are shown sorted from highest to lowest exposure. Click any volcano on the map to highlight its bar in the chart.
Convex hull + Population density by distance ring
GHSL (JRC) Convex hull + Concentric rings 0–50 km
Population density (persons/km²) in 50 concentric 1 km rings from the outer outline of the volcano (0–50 km). Density values were extracted from the GHSL gridded population raster. The interval slider merges rings into 2, 5, or 10 km buckets (arithmetic mean within each bucket). Blue dashed = median across all volcanoes; green dashed = arithmetic mean. Vertical markers show the ring of typical peak density.
VRR-style exposure score
OSM GHSL (JRC) Convex hull + Multi-criteria scoring
Inspired by the VRR (Volcanic Risk Ranking) framework of Nieto-torres et al. (2021). Each volcano is scored across 7 exposure categories: population (GHSL), residential buildings (GHSL), water infrastructure (OSM), transport (OSM), critical facilities (OSM), emergency services (OSM), and economic activity (OSM / land use). Raw counts per category are normalised to a 0–10 scale and summed into a composite score. Bars are sorted by total score.
⚠ Note: The VRR scores for Fani Moare and Oku Volcanic Field are based on point coordinates rather than convex hulls. Fani Moare is a submarine volcano situated ~2,500 m below sea level, making a surface convex hull delineation difficult. The Oku Volcanic Field is exceptionally large and dispersed; convex hulls for its vents have not been constructed.
Interactive volcano map
OpenStreetMap GVP / Smithsonian Point buffer Leaflet.js
Interactive map built with Leaflet.js on OpenStreetMap tiles. Buffer circles (10 / 30 / 100 km) are drawn around each summit. Standard mode: colour = last known eruption year from the Smithsonian GVP database (red ≥ 1900 CE; blue < 1900 CE; grey = unknown). Weighted exposure mode: marker size = 2025 weighted exposure (10 quantile classes); colour = relative population growth 1975→2100 (5 quantile classes). Clicking a marker updates all charts.
